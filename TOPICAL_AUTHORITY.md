# Topical Authority — safety.co.id

## Role and boundary

`safety.co.id` is an Indonesian occupational and building-safety reference plus an existing safety-equipment commerce site. Its primary readers are employers, site owners, workers, supervisors, HSE/K3 practitioners, procurement teams, facility managers, contractors, and designers working in Indonesia.

The knowledge hub may explain how to recognize obligations, organize competent review, ask better questions, and verify controls. It must not present a generic article as a site-specific risk assessment, legal opinion, engineering design, medical direction, rescue plan, permit approval, or authorization to perform hazardous work. High-risk work remains subject to the current law, the workplace's documented controls, manufacturer instructions, competent persons, and the authority having jurisdiction.

Commercial category, product, cart, checkout, account, quote, and contact intent remains owned by the existing `/shop/`, `/produk/`, protection-category routes, and transaction routes. Neutral articles may explain selection criteria and link contextually to relevant products, but cannot claim that buying PPE or equipment makes an uncontrolled task safe.

This plan deliberately does not create city-swapped pages. Geography deserves a page only when a real local authority, hazard profile, response arrangement, project case, or verified operational service changes the substance.

## Existing coverage and risks

The site has strong commercial breadth in PPE, fall protection, fire products, detectors, traffic equipment, marine equipment, and first-aid products. It has almost no reliable evidence of a governed, expert-reviewed K3 knowledge system. Existing “post” volume is overwhelmingly a location substitution pattern and must not be counted as topical authority.

Principal risks are:

- unsafe equivalence between a product and a complete control system;
- thousands of near-identical local pages without verified local operations;
- duplicate URL forms and sitemap disagreement;
- product and category pages competing with malformed generic sales posts;
- legacy product claims, availability, certificates, standards, and suitability that may be stale;
- high-stakes advice published without named competence, reviewer, revision date, or stop conditions;
- fire, electrical, lifting, confined-space, height, chemical, machinery, construction, health, first-aid, and emergency topics crossing different competent-person and regulator boundaries;
- obsolete citations, especially superseded P2K3 rules and revoked health regulations;
- archive and author pagination consuming crawl attention while adding little task value.

## Internal-link architecture

- Risk spine: SAF-02-01 is the method hub. Hazard-specific pages in SAF-05 through SAF-15 link back to it for hierarchy and risk-register logic without repeating the method.
- Work-control spine: SAF-04-01 links to energy isolation, height, hot-work/fire, lifting, confined-space, chemical, machinery, and construction planning pages only where the permit is an interface—not a substitute for the control.
- Learning loop: SAF-03 incident pages link to SAF-02 risk review, SAF-17 verification, SAF-16 competence, SAF-19 reporting culture, and SAF-20 evidence control.
- Procurement loop: selection articles link to SAF-18 due diligence and only then to contextually relevant `/shop/` categories or products. Product pages link back to neutral limitations/selection guidance.
- Emergency loop: every critical-hazard topic links to SAF-15-01 for emergency-plan governance and its own competent rescue/response owner; generic articles do not reproduce emergency procedures.
- Upward links: every article links to its parent topic hub; every parent hub links to all six children. Related links in the catalog are specific seeds, not a fixed widget.
- Orphan control: publication is blocked unless the page has an upward hub link, at least two contextually useful lateral links, and a valid return path from a hub or commercial category.

## Evidence and editorial standards

### Source hierarchy

1. Current primary Indonesian law and status metadata from official JDIH sources.
2. Official SNI issuer/catalog records and legitimately accessed standards text; do not reproduce paywalled standards.
3. Regulator, ministry, local fire service/authority, BNSP/licensing body, and sector-authority publications.
4. Manufacturer instructions, declarations, certificates, safety data sheets, inspection criteria, and recall notices for the exact product/model.
5. Site-specific engineering, occupational-hygiene, health, inspection, training, incident, and maintenance records with permission and limitations.
6. Secondary guidance only to explain context, never to overrule primary requirements.

### Mandatory publication gates

- **Gate 1 — source/status:** an editor records every primary source URL, official status, amendment/revocation check, access date, jurisdiction, and applicability assumptions. No exact clause, threshold, test interval, exposure limit, license, equipment class, or numerical rule is published from memory.
- **Gate 2 — K3 system:** an appropriately qualified Indonesian K3 reviewer checks SAF-01 through SAF-05 and SAF-16 through SAF-20 for role, governance, and control-system accuracy.
- **Gate 3 — discipline:** electrical, lifting, height, fire, chemical/hygiene, machinery, confined-space, construction, and emergency content receives a named reviewer competent for that discipline. “AK3” alone is not treated as universal competence.
- **Gate 4 — engineering/AHJ:** designs, calculations, load/path claims, protection settings, anchors, temporary works, fire strategy, egress, ventilation, rescue feasibility, or system acceptance require the responsible engineer/competent person and, where applicable, the authority having jurisdiction.
- **Gate 5 — health:** occupational-health, exposure-surveillance, ergonomics, first-aid, fitness, return-to-work, and psychosocial content receives occupational physician, nurse, hygienist, or ergonomist review appropriate to the claim. No diagnosis or treatment advice.
- **Gate 6 — field usability:** workers/supervisors from the intended audience test terminology, diagrams, language, accessibility, and stop/escalation cues. Reviewer comments and resolution are retained.
- **Gate 7 — release and expiry:** show author, reviewer names/roles, review date, next-review/change trigger, source register, assumptions, and correction channel. Material legal/manufacturer changes unpublish or flag affected claims until re-reviewed.

### Unsafe-content controls

- Never provide a universal permit, rescue plan, isolation procedure, lift plan, live-electrical procedure, confined-space entry authorization, fire response, chemical neutralization, medical treatment, or bypass method.
- Never imply PPE is the first or only control.
- Never tell an unqualified reader to test, repair, enter, energize, lift, climb, fight fire, rescue, or administer treatment.
- Use “stop work, isolate only if authorized and safe, keep people away, and contact the workplace's competent responsible person/emergency service” as the default escalation framing; tailor it only after expert review.
- Diagrams are conceptual and labeled as such. Worked examples use explicitly fictional data unless real, permissioned evidence exists.
- Product claims require exact manufacturer documentation and compatibility review. Marketplace badges, seller text, copied certificates, and visual similarity are not conformance proof.
