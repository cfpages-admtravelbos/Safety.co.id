## Purpose

This is the reusable evidence layer for the complete `safety.co.id` catalog. It researches the catalog as one connected management system so articles reuse primary sources without turning general education into a site risk assessment, permit, engineering design, rescue method, medical instruction, certificate, or declaration of compliance.

Writers must open and cite the original sources. This file records grounded starting points, applicability boundaries, and evidence still required before definitive claims.

## How writers must use this file

1. Start with the matching `SAF-*` coverage row and open every cited original source.
2. Recheck Indonesian legal status at outline time because Permenaker 11/2026 partially revoked provisions in several technical K3 regulations.
3. Separate governance, hazard assessment, engineered controls, work authorization, competence, inspection, emergency readiness, and records.
4. Never invent exposure limits, ratings, loads, distances, intervals, qualifications, permits, test results, rescue steps, medical treatment, or compliance.
5. Prefer elimination and collective controls before administrative controls and PPE.
6. Stop at the evidence gates for a real site, person, chemical, machine, lifting configuration, electrical system, confined space, height system, fire strategy, or emergency.

## Evidence scale

| Grade | Meaning | Permitted use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Current Indonesian law, regulator record, or official primary document | Establish public status, scope, definitions, and visible duties |
| B | ILO, WHO, ISO, standards-body, or government technical source | Explain systems, mechanisms, and evidence methods within stated limits |
| C | Manufacturer or recognized industry source | Explain one product/system only when identity and edition match |
| Gate | Site-, task-, product-, person-, competence-, or jurisdiction-specific evidence unresolved | Do not publish a definitive number, procedure, safety, legal, or performance claim |

- **Grounded facts:** Applicability depends on the actual workplace, activity, people, equipment, material, and implementing rules; a web article cannot determine compliance.

### KR-03 — SMK3 is a management system, not a certificate claim

### KR-05 — Hazard assessment and hierarchy of controls

- **Sources:** [ILO controlling risks](https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/occupational-safety-and-health-guide-labour-inspectors-and-other/how-can-occupational-safety-and-health-be-managed/controlling-risks), [ILO five-step risk-assessment guide](https://www.ilo.org/publications/5-step-guide-employers-workers-and-their-representatives-conducting).
- **Grade:** B.
- **Purpose:** Ground a lean, repeatable risk cycle rather than paperwork volume or PPE-first advice.
- **Summary:** Identify hazards and affected people, evaluate risk, select controls with priority at source, assign action, record, monitor, and update.
- **Grounded facts:** A generic matrix does not determine likelihood, consequence, exposure, acceptability, or residual risk for a site.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `SAF-02` and as the control spine for `SAF-04`–`SAF-15`, `SAF-18`, and `SAF-19`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Complex or high-consequence risks require discipline-specific methods, evidence, competence, and approval.

### KR-06 — Incident evidence and corrective learning

- **Sources:** [ILO investigation guide](https://www.ilo.org/publications/investigation-occupational-accidents-and-diseases), [ISO 45001 overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/63787.html).
- **Grade:** B.
- **Purpose:** Separate rescue/notification, evidence preservation, causation, corrective action, and effectiveness review.
- **Summary:** Investigation should learn from event sequences, conditions, controls, and organizational contributors rather than stop at blame labels.
- **Grounded facts:** A near miss, unsafe condition, injury, occupational disease, regulator report, insurance record, and internal learning case can have different definitions and pathways.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `SAF-03`, `SAF-17`, `SAF-19`, and `SAF-20`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not delay aid, disturb a controlled scene, determine fault, invent a case, or publish personal data; use current Indonesian notification requirements.

### KR-07 — Permit-to-work coordinates interfaces but does not make work safe

- **Sources:** [ILO Safety and Health in Construction code](https://www.ilo.org/publications/safety-and-health-construction-revised-edition), [ISO 45001 overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/63787.html).
- **Grade:** B/Gate.
- **Purpose:** Ground issuer/receiver roles, supporting controls, field verification, change, suspension, closeout, and handback.
- **Summary:** A permit is one administrative coordination layer; each hazard still needs suitable technical controls and competent execution.
- **Grounded facts:** Permit scope, task assessment, isolation, testing, simultaneous operations, authorization, handover, and restoration are different checks.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `SAF-04`, with interfaces to `SAF-06`–`SAF-13` and `SAF-15`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Never provide a universal form, mandatory list, validity time, isolation sequence, gas criterion, or authorization from generic research.

### KR-08 — PPE remains the last control layer

## Cross-catalog fact bank

1. A management system, legal duty, risk assessment, engineered control, permit, competence record, inspection, and audit prove different things.
2. Control selection starts with eliminating or controlling the hazard at source; PPE does not repair a weak design.
3. A generic article cannot approve a real site, task, worker, product, chemical, machine, lift, electrical system, confined space, height system, or fire strategy.
4. Indonesian technical K3 status must be rechecked against Permenaker 11/2026 before quoting provisions.
5. A certificate image does not prove issuer, current status, scope, identity match, delivered product, installation quality, or system performance.
6. Emergency planning must match credible scenarios, people, accessibility, on-site capability, communications, external responders, and drills.
7. An exposure observation is not a measured assessment; a measured exposure is not a diagnosis or fitness decision.
8. Activity counts and injury rates alone do not establish control effectiveness or culture.
9. Controlled documents direct current work; records preserve evidence of what occurred.
10. Product, service, price, credential, client, case, warranty, and outcome claims require dated operator-approved evidence.
